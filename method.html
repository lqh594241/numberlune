A reading should never hide its arithmetic. Numberlune uses a component-reduction convention for life paths and a single-digit calendar-year convention for personal years. These are numerology traditions, not scientific measurements.

How to calculate your life path number

  1. Write down your actual birth month, day and full year.
  2. Reduce each component by adding its digits until it is 1–9, 11, 22 or 33.
  3. Add the three reduced components.
  4. Reduce the total again, stopping at 1–9, 11, 22 or 33.

Example: July 18, 1990

Month: 7

Day: 18 → 1 + 8 = 9

Year: 1990 → 1 + 9 + 9 + 0 = 19 → 10 → 1

Together: 7 + 9 + 1 = 17 → 8

Life path: 8 / The Steward

When do master numbers appear?

We preserve 11, 22 and 33 during each component reduction and during the final reduction. For February 8, 1990: month 2 + day 8 + year 1 = 11. The result remains 11, with 2 as its single-digit companion.

For February 11, 1980: 2 + 11 + 9 = 22. For September 22, 1982: 9 + 22 + 2 = 33. Some traditions preserve only 11 and 22, and some reduce all results to single digits. None of these conventions makes a person more valuable or capable.

Why can different calculators disagree?

Preserving master numbers changes where reduction stops. For example, November 11, 2009 gives 11 + 11 + 11 = 33 under our method, while direct digit addition gives 15 → 6. This is a convention difference, not a hidden truth about which result is your destiny.

Personal year: a different calculation

Reduce the birth month, birth day and chosen calendar year to single digits, add them, then reduce the total to 1–9. We do not preserve master numbers here. For July 18 in 2026: 7 + 9 + 1 = 17 → 8. The reading applies as a symbolic theme from January 1 through December 31. Leap-day birthdays are accepted even in non-leap reading years.

Name number: Pythagorean A–Z values

Map A–I to 1–9, J–R to 1–9 and S–Z to 1–8. Add all letter values, then reduce the total, preserving 11, 22 and 33. See the complete chart and normalization rules.

Compatibility: a comparison, not a score

We calculate two life paths independently and compare their editorial themes. For a master number, we also show its single-digit companion. We do not calculate a success percentage, rank relationships or claim to predict attraction, conflict or longevity.

What we validate, and what validation means

The app rejects dates that do not exist, future birthdays and unsupported name characters. Leap-day birthdays remain valid when exploring a non-leap personal year. All 12 guide examples are checked when pages are generated; automated tests also exercise the calculation rules.

These checks establish consistency with our documented method. They do not validate numerology as a predictive system. Read the editorial policy and source notes for the distinction.

A short glossary

Reduction
Adding digits repeatedly until the stated stopping rule is reached.
Master number
11, 22 or 33 when preserved by our convention. This is a calculation label, not a ranking.
Single-digit companion
The result of reducing a master number without preservation: 11/2, 22/4 and 33/6.
Expression number
The name number formed by adding the mapped values of all letters. It differs from vowel-only or consonant-only readings.

What these methods cannot tell you

Numerology has no established scientific validity as a personality test or predictive tool. A calculation cannot determine your health, finances, future, relationships or worth. Treat descriptions as reflection prompts and use evidence and qualified advice for consequential decisions.
